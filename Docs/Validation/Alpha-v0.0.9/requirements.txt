# SECTION 2 — FUNDAMENTAL TERRAIN CORRECTIONS

## 2.1 Remove maze-like terrain patterns

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

Current worlds contain unnatural, repetitive terrain formations.

Observed defects:

- Maze-like mountain ridges.
- Unexplained narrow trenches.
- Repeating straight lines.
- Rectangular terrain formations.
- Sharp 90-degree turns.
- Narrow ridges with almost no width.
- Sudden cliff walls.
- Repetitive grassland waves.
- Artificial-looking valleys.
- Biome transitions with repeated geometric patterns.

These artifacts appear across several geographical regions.

Investigate continental masks, plate-boundary elevation fields, erosion, terrain interpolation, drainage carving, and mesh construction.

Distinguish geometry defects from material and shader artifacts using diagnostic rendering.

Correct the underlying procedural algorithms rather than hiding artifacts with textures.

Terrain must flow naturally between geological formations.

## 2.2 Rebuild mountain proportions

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

Mountains currently appear too small, too narrow, and insufficiently imposing compared with the enormous world scale.

Requirements:

- Massive mountain ranges spanning substantial distances.
- Long, connected mountain chains.
- Broad mountain bases.
- Naturally connected foothills.
- Increasing elevations toward major summits.
- Distinct peaks and ridgelines.
- Large mountain valleys.
- Smooth transitions into nearby terrain.
- Appropriate geological erosion.
- Realistic traversal routes and mountain passes.

Mountains must be dramatically more noticeable from high altitude and during world overview.

Investigate why model elevations above 10,000 meters still appear visually unimpressive.

Correct the mountain-to-world proportions, relief distribution, and rendering.

The desired visual increase may be an order of magnitude, but do not blindly multiply every mountain's altitude by 10 or 20.

Avoid exaggerated elevation values that break climate, collision, camera behavior, or world traversal.

Mountains should be imposing without making the entire world inaccessible.

## 2.3 Add major plateaus

**PRIORITY: HIGH**

Plateaus should become common, recognizable geological features where appropriate.

Generate:

- Grassland plateaus.
- Forested plateaus.
- Desert mesas.
- Rocky tablelands.
- Mountain plateaus.
- Coastal plateaus.
- Major escarpments.

Plateaus should contain broad, elevated, relatively level terrain.

Some should extend across many kilometers.

Their edges should include appropriate slopes, erosion valleys, and occasional cliffs.

Plateaus must be integrated into geology and erosion rather than applied as arbitrary terrain stamps.

## 2.4 Add coastal cliffs and ocean-facing plateaus

Not every coastline should slope gently toward the ocean.

Generate a natural distribution of:

- Sandy beaches.
- Rocky coasts.
- Tall coastal cliffs.
- Raised headlands.
- Coastal plateaus.
- Steep ocean-facing escarpments.
- Protected coves.
- Gradual shoreline slopes.

Some plateaus should extend directly to cliffs overlooking the ocean.

Ocean depth and shoreline geometry must correspond correctly to these features.

## 2.5 Rework grasslands

**PRIORITY: HIGH**

Current grasslands are excessively wavy and resemble sand dunes.

Grassland generation must favor:

- Vast open plains.
- Extensive relatively flat areas.
- Gentle rolling hills.
- Subtle local elevation variation.
- Natural rivers and streams.
- Occasional hills and ridges.
- Scattered trees.
- Small groves.
- Wetlands in suitable regions.
- Gradual transitions to forests and highlands.

Preserve the attractive rolling dune geography already present in deserts.

Grasslands and deserts must not share identical terrain profiles.

Grasslands should feel expansive without becoming empty or repetitive.

## 2.6 Improve biome transitions

Desert-to-grassland and other biome boundaries should not feature unnatural repeated ridge patterns or abrupt rectangular transitions.

Use broad geographical ecotones.

Blend appropriate vegetation, soil, ground coloration, and climate over meaningful distances.

Different biomes should retain recognizable identities while connecting naturally.

Restore natural green grassland colors rather than excessively orange or brown vegetation.

# SECTION 3 — RIVERS, LAKES, AND COASTLINES

## 3.1 Repair river geometry and missing water

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

Many rivers currently appear as dry trenches or hairline cracks.

Some channels have straight segments, strange turns, abrupt endings, and excessive incision.

Requirements:

- Continuous downhill river paths.
- Connected drainage networks.
- Proper tributary confluences.
- Natural meanders.
- Flow-dependent widths.
- Appropriate channel depths.
- Reliable water-surface placement.
- Smooth river-to-lake transitions.
- Realistic river mouths.

Permanent rivers must visibly contain water.

Only seasonally dry or ephemeral channels may be empty, where supported by climate and hydrology.

Investigate whether some apparent river trenches originate from continental masks or elevation algorithms rather than actual river generation.

Do not confuse ocean-separation cuts with freshwater rivers.

## 3.2 Improve lakes and eliminate excessive mountain pools

Current worlds contain too many strange water pools inside mountain formations.

Generate lakes based on plausible drainage basins.

Reduce unexplained elevated ponds, small random mountain lakes, and water-filled geometric holes.

Preserve natural alpine lakes where justified by terrain and climate.

Every permanent lake should have a sensible water budget and drainage relationship.

## 3.3 Smooth shoreline geometry

**PRIORITY: HIGH**

The existing coastline and lake-island boundaries frequently appear blocky.

Fix the actual shoreline mesh and triangulation.

Requirements:

- Smoother naturally curved shorelines.
- Improved shoreline sampling.
- No obvious square or polygonal edges.
- Stable boundaries across terrain LOD changes.
- Natural bays and small peninsulas.
- Gradual banks in appropriate locations.
- Realistic transitions from sand or earth to water.

Do not make every shoreline uniformly rounded.

## 3.4 Improve shallow-water transparency

Shallow water should reveal the underwater terrain.

Implement:

- Clear shallow-water visibility near shore.
- Smooth transparency transitions.
- Visible sand, gravel, and submerged rocks.
- Gradual opacity increases with depth.
- Turquoise shallow water where appropriate.
- Natural transitions into deeper calm blue.

Ponds and shallow lakes should generally retain attractive light blue or turquoise coloration where conditions permit.

Avoid excessively dark, nearly black water.

## 3.5 Preserve negative underwater elevation

Sea level must not be the minimum world elevation.

Support actual underwater bathymetry extending below zero elevation.

Underwater terrain should include:

- Continental shelves.
- Sloping coastal seabeds.
- Deeper ocean basins.
- Underwater ridges.
- Submerged valleys.
- Varied shallow-water terrain.

This forms the foundation for future coral reefs, kelp forests, seagrass, fish, marine wildlife, diving, and underwater exploration.

Full marine ecological simulation is not required in this milestone, but its geographic foundation must not be compromised.

## 3.6 Improve ocean appearance

Preserve the preferred calm blue water aesthetic.

Reduce excessively choppy, repetitive waves.

Weather should influence wave strength appropriately.

Prevent color seams, harsh water geometry boundaries, and flickering at long viewing distances.

# SECTION 4 — SNOW, ICE, AND ATMOSPHERE

## 4.1 Correct snow distribution

Snow should depend on actual climate and elevation.

Evaluate:

- Latitude.
- Air temperature.
- Elevation.
- Atmospheric lapse rate.
- Seasonal conditions.
- Precipitation.
- Terrain exposure.

Avoid applying snow automatically to every mountain regardless of local temperature.

Extremely tall tropical mountains may still have snow where climate conditions justify it.

Avoid excessive white outlines following narrow mountain ridges.

## 4.2 Improve frozen lakes

Frozen water should look like pale, naturally reflective ice.

Use:

- Very light blue-white coloration.
- Subtle reflective highlights.
- Restrained surface texture.
- Gentle variation.
- Optional snow accumulation.

Remove the overly textured denim-like appearance.

## 4.3 Fix flickering black snow specks

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

Snow currently contains dark or black specks that appear and disappear when the camera moves.

Investigate:

- Shadow bias.
- Shadow acne.
- Z-fighting.
- Incorrect normals.
- Overlapping surfaces.
- Shader instability.
- Depth precision.
- LOD transitions.
- Vegetation or mesh clipping.

Identify the actual cause using diagnostic render modes.

Do not conceal the problem with brighter snow.

Snow must remain stable when viewed from different angles.

## 4.4 Stabilize clouds

Clouds currently sometimes appear too low, move incorrectly, or change position when zooming.

Clouds must:

- Remain anchored to world coordinates.
- Move continuously with wind.
- Form and dissipate gradually.
- Have natural shapes.
- Remain stable during zoom.
- Avoid square edges and procedural noise-sheet appearances.
- Occupy appropriate atmospheric altitudes.
- Interact naturally with regional weather.

Rain clouds should darken during storms.

Cloud coverage and precipitation must correspond geographically.

Preserve independent weather and cloud display controls.

# SECTION 5 — CAVES, RAVINES, AND MINING

## 5.1 Add natural ravines

Generate geological ravines, gorges, and canyons.

They should result from appropriate erosion or geological processes.

Features may include:

- Narrow natural gorges.
- River-cut canyons.
- Desert ravines.
- Mountain valleys.
- Plateau-cut canyons.
- Steep rocky passages.

These must not resemble the existing artificial maze-like trenches.

## 5.2 Add spaghetti-like cave networks

**PRIORITY: MAJOR NEW FEATURE**

Implement genuinely three-dimensional procedural caves.

Cave systems should include:

- Winding passages.
- Branching tunnels.
- Multiple depths.
- Interconnected loops.
- Large caverns.
- Narrow corridors.
- Vertical shafts.
- Hillside entrances.
- Cliff entrances.
- Connections to natural ravines.

Caves should not resemble flat two-dimensional mazes.

Investigate appropriate volumetric terrain or local underground mesh architecture.

A standard heightfield alone cannot represent true underground tunnels.

Consider local voxel systems, signed-distance fields, or other technically suitable approaches.

Use deterministic generation and scalable streaming.

Integrate caves with the geological model.

Do not generate full-detail underground meshes for the entire Colossal world at once.

## 5.3 Expand terrain mining

Players should be able to excavate accessible solid ground, including:

- Grasslands.
- Deserts.
- Hillsides.
- Mountains.
- Cliffs.
- Rock formations.
- Underground terrain.

Allow excavations to form holes, tunnels, shafts, and chambers.

Terrain modifications must persist after saving and loading.

Mining should remove actual geological material rather than merely lowering the visible surface.

## 5.4 Add excavation targeting circle

**PRIORITY: HIGH**

Implement a visible circular excavation indicator.

The targeting circle should:

- Follow the player's aim.
- Highlight the actual dig location.
- Show the excavation radius.
- Conform to surface geometry.
- Work on horizontal and vertical surfaces.
- Remain visible against snow, earth, sand, and rock.
- Indicate invalid excavation targets.

The actual excavation must match the displayed indicator.

## 5.5 Repair hairy and grainy excavation surfaces

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

Current excavated holes have dark, grainy, hairy-looking interiors.

Investigate geometry, normals, material assignment, z-fighting, shadow artifacts, and terrain-mesh replacement.

Excavated surfaces should use suitable earth or rock materials.

Shallow holes should reveal soil.

Deeper excavation should expose geological strata.

Remove black speckling, flickering, and jagged mesh artifacts.

## 5.6 Stop excavation-induced character shaking

Digging currently causes violent character and camera jitter.

Investigate character grounding, collision updates, terrain replacement, collider synchronization, and camera movement.

The character must remain stable during excavation.

Test digging on level ground, slopes, near hole edges, and underground.

# SECTION 6 — PLAYER MOVEMENT AND PHYSICS

## 6.1 Eliminate character vibration

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

The character currently vibrates visibly during standing, walking, digging, and flying.

Investigate:

- Physics/render update synchronization.
- Character-controller grounding.
- Collider overlap.
- Animation transforms.
- Camera-follow logic.
- Terrain LOD replacement.
- World-coordinate precision.
- Floating-point instability.

Correct the root cause.

Do not simply hide vibration with visual smoothing.

## 6.2 Fix high-speed flight

**PRIORITY: CRITICAL**

High-speed flight causes violent character jitter.

Flight must remain smooth during:

- Maximum-speed movement.
- Sudden acceleration.
- Deceleration.
- Sharp turns.
- Rapid altitude changes.
- High-altitude travel.
- Colossal-world navigation.

Maintain stable camera behavior and character animation.

## 6.3 Double-Space must initiate freefall

**PRIORITY: HIGH**

Double-tapping Space currently enables or disables flight.

Preserve that control.

However, disabling flight while airborne currently teleports the character to the ground.

This must be fixed.

When the player double-taps Space during flight:

1. Disable powered flight.
2. Keep the character at the current airborne position.
3. Preserve appropriate momentum.
4. Activate gravity.
5. Transition into a falling animation.
6. Allow the character to fall naturally.
7. Detect collision with the ground.
8. Transition into the landing animation.

Do not teleport the character downward.

Do not snap the player instantly to a grounded state.

Fall damage is not required yet.

## 6.4 Improve movement animations

Implement distinct animations for:

- Idle.
- Walking.
- Running.
- Jumping.
- Flying.
- Freefall.
- Landing.
- Digging.

During freefall, the character should visibly move their arms and legs in a controlled flailing or running-in-the-air animation.

Blend animation states smoothly.

Animations must not cause unintended changes in the player's physical position.
