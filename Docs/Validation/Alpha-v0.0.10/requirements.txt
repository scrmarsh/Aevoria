# MISSION

Transform Aevoria from a largely uninhabited procedural landscape into the beginning of a living, populated, historically evolving world.

Introduce the foundational systems for civilizations, settlements, populations, resource economies, territorial expansion, political entities, and world history.

These systems must become the foundation for future kingdoms, armies, wars, diplomacy, family dynasties, and fully simulated societies.

Also replace the temporary character architecture with an expandable humanoid framework and include the previously specified secret doctor Easter egg.

**The civilization simulation is the primary purpose of this update. The doctor is a secondary character feature.**

# PHASE 2 — CIVILIZATION GENERATION

Aevoria must begin supporting procedurally generated civilizations.

The long-term goal is for civilizations to emerge, expand, compete, develop cultures, and produce their own histories.

For this release, implement the foundational simulation.

## 2.1 Civilization identities

Each generated civilization should have:

- Unique persistent ID.
- Procedurally generated name.
- Distinct identifying color.
- Origin location.
- Population.
- Settlement collection.
- Territorial influence.
- Available resources.
- Development level.
- Political identity.
- Cultural identity data.
- Historical record.

Names must be sufficiently varied to avoid excessive repetition.

Civilization colors should remain consistent across sessions and save/load.

## 2.2 Geographic suitability

Civilizations should not spawn arbitrarily.

Evaluate terrain suitability using:

- Elevation.
- Slope.
- Climate.
- Freshwater accessibility.
- Nearby food resources.
- Habitable land.
- Biome type.
- Temperature.
- Other available geographic information.

Favor plausible locations such as fertile valleys, rivers, grasslands, and suitable coastal regions.

Avoid spawning ordinary settlements:

- Beneath oceans.
- On extreme cliff faces.
- Inside inaccessible terrain.
- On permanently submerged land.
- At positions that cannot be restored after world loading.

Allow the system to evolve later to support mountain, desert, coastal, and other specialized civilizations.

## 2.3 Civilization separation

Generated civilizations should have reasonable geographic spacing.

Avoid clustering every civilization into a single small region unless the geography meaningfully supports dense settlement.

Support worlds with multiple independent population centers.

Scale the number of generated civilizations according to world size and available habitable area.

Expose generation parameters for testing.

# PHASE 3 — PROCEDURAL SETTLEMENTS

Introduce functioning settlements.

Initially support foundational settlement categories such as:

- Small camps.
- Villages.
- Towns.
- Emerging regional centers.

A settlement is not merely a decorative label.

It must have simulation data.

Each settlement should maintain:

- Name.
- Geographic position.
- Population.
- Parent civilization.
- Food stores.
- Resource stores.
- Development stage.
- Local territory.
- Growth rate.
- Founding date.
- Historical events.

## 3.1 Settlement placement

Use actual world geography to position settlements.

Settlements should favor usable terrain and appropriate proximity to resources.

Avoid buildings floating above the ground.

Avoid settlements intersecting steep terrain or water surfaces.

Integrate settlement positioning with geographic coordinates and world-origin rebasing.

## 3.2 Settlement rendering

Add an initial visual settlement representation.

At minimum, provide a recognizable cluster of buildings or structures.

Initial procedural building types may include:

- Simple dwellings.
- Storage buildings.
- Communal structures.
- Resource-production structures.

Use inexpensive reusable meshes or prefabs.

The art may be relatively simple for Alpha v0.0.10, but settlements should look like intentional inhabited locations rather than random colored cubes.

Settlement rendering should scale with development.

A tiny camp should look different from a growing town.

## 3.3 Terrain integration

Buildings should adapt to local slopes where possible.

Flatten only small construction footprints when necessary.

Do not destructively reshape major geological features to force settlements into inappropriate terrain.

Avoid corrupting rivers, shorelines, caves, and excavation data.

## 3.4 Settlement growth

Settlements should change over simulated time.

When resources and population permit:

- Population increases.
- Additional structures appear.
- Settlement footprints expand.
- Development level rises.

When conditions become unfavorable:

- Growth may slow.
- Resources may decline.
- Population may shrink.
- Settlements may enter decline.

Avoid allowing all settlements to grow endlessly regardless of available resources.

# PHASE 4 — POPULATION SIMULATION

Implement aggregate population simulation as the initial foundation.

Each settlement should have a population quantity affected by:

- Food availability.
- Resource constraints.
- Environmental suitability.
- Births.
- Deaths.
- Migration where supported.
- Settlement capacity.

Do not require an individual rendered person for every simulated inhabitant.

Use aggregate simulation for large populations.

The population system must scale to large worlds.

## 4.1 Population development

Population growth should be gradual and influenced by settlement conditions.

Avoid:

- Instant exponential growth.
- Negative population values.
- Populations increasing without food.
- Settlements supporting absurd populations despite having no resources.

## 4.2 Migration

If feasible, support simple population transfers.

Examples:

- People move toward better-supported settlements.
- Crowded settlements lose some population.
- Declining settlements may experience emigration.

Maintain conservation of population where the modeled mechanism requires it.

## 4.3 Individual citizens — foundational architecture

Prepare for future individual-level inhabitants.

Define an extensible population representation supporting future:

- Individual identity.
- Age.
- Household.
- Occupation.
- Social relationships.
- Family lineage.
- Leadership roles.

Do not build a fully simulated personal life for every citizen in this milestone.

That is a future system.

The goal is an expandable, efficient foundation.

# PHASE 5 — RESOURCES AND EARLY ECONOMY

The world should contain geographically meaningful resources.

Use existing world-generation resource data where available.

Initial categories may include:

- Food.
- Timber.
- Stone.
- Ore or metal-bearing resources.
- Basic production supplies.

Do not introduce arbitrary inventories disconnected from the actual geography.

## 5.1 Resource distribution

Different biomes and terrain types should provide different resource opportunities.

For example:

Forests can provide timber.

Fertile land can support food production.

Rocky and mountainous terrain may provide stone and ore.

Rivers and coasts may support future fishing and trade systems.

Resource availability should be consistent with the generated world.

## 5.2 Settlement production

Settlements should produce resources based on:

- Nearby land.
- Population.
- Development.
- Available resource deposits.
- Production capacity.

Use a manageable aggregate production model initially.

## 5.3 Consumption

Settlements consume resources.

Food should be required for population support.

Construction and development should require suitable materials.

Inventories must persist between simulation ticks.

## 5.4 Resource shortage

Resource shortages should have consequences.

Possible effects:

- Reduced population growth.
- Stalled construction.
- Declining settlement prosperity.
- Migration.
- Population reduction during prolonged food scarcity.

Avoid implementing resource scarcity as a purely cosmetic statistic.

## 5.5 Trade foundation

Create a data model for future trade between settlements.

If time permits, implement a basic resource transfer mechanism between connected or geographically accessible settlements.

Do not pretend to have a complete trading economy if only inventory transfers are supported.

Preserve a clear distinction between implemented production, exchange, and future market systems.

# PHASE 6 — TERRITORIAL EXPANSION

- Civilization territory.
- Settlement influence.
- Unclaimed land.
- Territory growth.
- Territory contraction.

## 6.1 Territory growth

Expansion should consider:

- Distance.
- Geography.
- Existing settlements.
- Population strength.
- Development level.
- Neighboring territorial claims.

Avoid territory spreading equally across mountain ranges, oceans, and impassable features without geographic justification.

## 6.2 Political map

In Top-Down Mode, add a toggleable political territory overlay.

Requirements:

- Each civilization has a distinct color.
- Unclaimed land remains neutral.
- Overlay remains subtle and readable.
- Terrain remains visible underneath.
- Boundaries update as influence changes.
- Colors persist consistently across save/load.
- Overlay works across streamed terrain chunks.

Avoid abrupt seams between chunks.

Support zooming between local and global views.

## 6.3 Territory information

Selecting a civilization's territory should allow the player to inspect basic data.

Display:

- Civilization name.
- Population.
- Settlements.
- Controlled area.
- Development level.
- Major resources.

Do not invent values in the user interface; read them from active simulation state.

Alpha v0.0.9 establishes the natural world.
