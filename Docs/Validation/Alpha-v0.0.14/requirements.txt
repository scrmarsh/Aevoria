# AEVORIA — ALPHA v0.0.14
## PROJECT GENESIS
### Procedural Materials, Natural Surfaces & Compact Asset Architecture

**Development platform:** Unity 6.3 LTS / URP / Windows  
**Previous release:** Alpha v0.0.13 — The Mediterranean Update  
**Update type:** Major rendering and asset-generation overhaul  
**Priority:** Visual quality → Terrain integration → Performance → Audio → Storage optimization

---

## 1. Primary Mission

Implement a high-quality procedural material system that fundamentally improves how Aevoria's world looks.

Aevoria must no longer rely on terrain surfaces that resemble repeated square textures, flat painted colors, stretched mountain materials, or simplistic noise patterns.

The objective is to achieve richly detailed, geographically believable terrain using **compact mathematical material recipes instead of large collections of traditional texture files**.

The player's experience must resemble a professionally developed 3D world, not a procedurally colored heightmap.

**The most important requirement is that the world must look natural from every viewing distance.**

This includes the highest overhead world view, intermediate camera distances, and ground-level exploration.

## 2. Preservation and Baseline Verification

Before modifying the project:

1. Read the latest development checkpoint and validation documentation.
2. Confirm the current source version and most recent completed release.
3. Preserve all existing builds, archives, player saves, and historical generation products.
4. Preserve the existing Mediterranean geography, tectonics, mountains, rivers, lakes, climate, biomes, villages, caves, characters, controls, and persistence.
5. Do not overwrite older release builds.
6. Do not regenerate existing player worlds silently.
7. Keep material-generation changes compatible with the existing geography whenever possible.
8. Record the baseline build size, asset usage, performance, and representative screenshots.

Do not assume v0.0.13 is fully validated solely from its version number. Verify its final checkpoint and maintain compatibility with the actual completed baseline.

Do not modify Dr. House or any other protected character assets unless an unrelated critical issue makes it absolutely necessary.

Do not spawn additional agents without authorization.

## 3. Aevoria's New Procedural Material Architecture

Create a modular procedural material system that allows materials to be defined through compact recipes.

A material recipe should support:

- Deterministic procedural seed.
- Base color gradient.
- Multiple noise functions.
- Noise scale and frequency.
- Fractal octave configuration.
- Domain warping.
- Material roughness.
- Surface normal variation.
- Geological strata.
- Cracks and fractures.
- Erosion and weathering.
- Moisture response.
- Temperature response.
- Snow accumulation.
- Moss and vegetation masks.
- Large-scale color variation.
- Localized surface detail.
- Distance-based filtering.
- Biome and geological compatibility.

Each recipe must be reusable, versioned, and reproducible.

Use Unity URP-compatible shader implementations and efficient GPU rendering where appropriate.

Avoid requiring full-resolution generated image files for every terrain location.

## 4. Mandatory Anti-Tiling Requirement

**Absolutely no visibly repeated square terrain textures.**

Forbidden results include:

- Checkerboard patterns.
- Square material boundaries.
- Repeated identical rock patches.
- Repeating grass stamps.
- Obvious UV tiling.
- Stretched cliff textures.
- Blurry material squares.
- Pixelated biome transitions.
- Static-like procedural noise.
- Noticeably identical surface patterns over large distances.

Implement several complementary anti-tiling techniques rather than relying on a single noise filter.

Required techniques where appropriate:

1. World-space material coordinates.
2. Triplanar projection on steep terrain.
3. Multi-octave noise.
4. Domain-warped material sampling.
5. Large-scale geographic color variation.
6. Medium-scale surface breakup.
7. Small-scale detail variation.
8. Noise-distorted material transition masks.
9. Stochastic or rotated sampling for any retained repeated source textures.
10. Distance-aware mipmapping, derivative filtering, or equivalent antialiasing.

The world must not reveal material repetition when the camera pans, rotates, zooms, or travels across chunk boundaries.

Procedural materials should be continuous across terrain chunks and streaming regions.

## 5. Geographic Material Intelligence

Material distribution must be driven by the existing simulation data rather than arbitrary color selection.

Inputs should include:

- Elevation.
- Slope.
- Slope aspect.
- Geological classification.
- Temperature.
- Moisture.
- Rainfall.
- Wind exposure.
- Distance to oceans.
- Distance to rivers and lakes.
- Snow conditions.
- Biome.
- Terrain curvature and erosion.
- Regional geographic variation.

For example:

A steep mountain face should expose more rock.

Sheltered ledges should collect soil, snow, or vegetation when conditions permit.

Lower foothills should transition into grass, shrubs, stones, and soil.

Wet riverbanks should display darker ground, vegetation, sediment, and appropriate transitions.

Dry desert regions should show sand accumulation, exposed bedrock, and weathered stone.

Material logic must remain deterministic for the same world seed and generation version.

## 6. Procedural Rock and Stone

Develop multiple distinct rock material families.

Examples:

- Mediterranean limestone.
- Weathered sandstone.
- Northern granite.
- Dark metamorphic rock.
- Layered sedimentary stone.
- Jagged mountain bedrock.
- River-smoothed stone.
- Cave limestone.

Each material must support detailed color variation, fractures, sediment layers, micro-grain, erosion, surface relief, and roughness.

Rock appearance must vary across entire mountains, not merely across individual texture samples.

Geological strata should follow plausible regional directions rather than appearing randomly painted onto the terrain.

Triplanar projection must prevent materials from stretching down cliff faces.

Normal detail must respond naturally to lighting.

Where substantial silhouette-changing relief is required, use actual geometry rather than relying on normal maps alone.

## 7. Grassland and Mediterranean Ground

Create richly detailed procedural ground materials for temperate and Mediterranean environments.

Required components:

- Multiple green and yellow grass tones.
- Exposed soil.
- Dry grass.
- Small stones.
- Rocky patches.
- Natural erosion patterns.
- Moss.
- Sparse vegetation regions.
- Soil moisture variation.
- Transition regions around pathways and settlements.

The ground must contain coherent patches of different materials rather than uniformly distributed multicolored noise.

Grassland hills should remain readable from distant world views.

Up close, fine-scale material details and actual vegetation geometry must become visible.

## 8. Forest Floor Materials

Create dedicated forest-floor materials for northern and temperate forests.

Support:

- Dark soil.
- Pine needles.
- Leaf litter.
- Moss.
- Small stones.
- Organic debris.
- Damp earth.
- Exposed roots where suitable.
- Lichen.
- Decaying vegetation.

These materials should respond to forest density, moisture, canopy cover, altitude, and temperature.

Do not simply recolor ordinary grass to represent forest ground.

Preserve the existing forest distribution system.

## 9. Desert and Sand Materials

Develop several realistic desert surface families:

- Fine windblown sand.
- Rippled dune sand.
- Compact dry sand.
- Rocky desert.
- Weathered sandstone.
- Dry cracked soil.
- White salt flats.
- Oasis soil.
- River-deposited sediment.

Large dunes must retain their actual geographic geometry.

Procedural materials provide surface appearance, not a substitute for dunes, cliffs, mesas, or other important landforms.

Sand ripples should have plausible directional structure influenced by prevailing wind.

The desert must not resemble yellow static or a repeated noise texture.

## 10. Snow, Ice, and Arctic Materials

Create natural cold-region surfaces:

- Fresh snow.
- Wind-packed snow.
- Dirty melting snow.
- Exposed alpine rock.
- Ice.
- Frozen soil.
- Permafrost.
- Snow-covered forest ground.

Snow must accumulate according to climate, elevation, slope, exposure, and available terrain geometry.

Transitions between snow and exposed rock should be irregular and geographically believable.

Avoid artificial white bands or uniformly painted mountains.

## 11. Seamless Material Blending

Develop a consistent blending architecture supporting multiple material influences.

Examples:

- Rock → Scree → Soil → Grass.
- Forest floor → Moss → Rock.
- Grass → Dirt → Sand.
- Sand → Rock → Salt flat.
- Alpine rock → Ice → Snow.
- Dry riverbank → Damp soil → Wet sediment.

Transitions should use meaningful terrain and environment masks enhanced by controlled procedural variation.

Do not allow chunk boundaries, grid cells, or material assignment regions to become visibly apparent.

Test transitions on varied slopes and complex shorelines.

## 12. Surface Depth and Lighting

Procedural materials must support realistic physically based shading.

Implement or derive where useful:

- Base color.
- Normal detail.
- Roughness or smoothness.
- Ambient occlusion where appropriate.
- Height or displacement-related detail.
- Surface wetness.
- Snow or dust accumulation.

Avoid expensive techniques when they provide little visual improvement.

Optimize material detail according to viewing distance and screen-space visibility.

World Mode must not become noisy or oversharpened when displaying large regions.

Close-up Explorer Mode must not look flat.

## 13. Three Levels of Surface Detail

Implement a consistent scale-aware system.

**Macro detail — regional**

Controls large color regions, geological variation, vegetation density, and broad surface differences.

**Meso detail — landscape**

Controls patches of exposed rock, dirt, grass, erosion, sediment, moss, and weathered surfaces.

**Micro detail — close-up**

Controls cracks, grain, small pebbles, grooves, and material roughness.

These three scales must work together without creating excessive visual noise.

Avoid expensive microscopic calculations at distances where their details cannot be resolved.

## 14. Actual Geometry and Environmental Detail

Procedural texturing alone is insufficient.

Use efficient geometry and instancing for appropriate objects such as:

- Stones and boulders.
- Rock fragments.
- Grass clumps.
- Shrubs.
- Flowers.
- Fallen branches.
- Logs.
- Scree deposits.
- Forest litter.
- Tree roots.
- Coastal stones.

Placement should depend on geography, slope, climate, soil, moisture, and vegetation suitability.

Use deterministic object placement and distance-based level of detail.

Maintain stable visibility during camera movement.

Avoid obvious repeated object arrangements.

## 15. Asset Size and Installation Goals

The preferred installed game size is **2 GB or less**, with **10 GB as the upper planning limit**.

These are development goals, not requirements that justify sacrificing visual fidelity or breaking existing functionality.

Prefer:

- Compact procedural material recipes.
- Reusable shaders.
- Reusable geometry.
- Deterministic seeds.
- Instanced meshes.
- Efficient asset compression.
- Shared material libraries.
- Optional generated caches.

Avoid shipping thousands of large redundant textures.

Keep generated world data and persistent saves separate from the core game installation.

Measure and report:

- Compressed distribution size.
- Installed application size.
- Procedural material data size.
- Shader and supporting asset size.
- Optional cache size.
- Typical world save size.
- Peak graphics memory consumption.

Do not make unverified claims about storage savings.

## 16. Procedural Environmental Audio

After the visual material system is stable, investigate compact procedural audio.

Prototype:

- Wind.
- Rain.
- Ocean waves.
- River ambience.
- Footsteps.
- Mining impacts.
- Simple interface sounds.

Use synthesis, filtered noise, compact parameters, and reusable sound generators where appropriate.

For example, wind may be generated through filtered noise modulated by wind strength and environmental conditions.

Footsteps may vary according to terrain material.

Mining impacts may vary according to rock classification.

Use conventional compressed audio where procedural synthesis would sound noticeably worse.

Do not remove or damage existing audio systems.

Avoid unnecessary CPU expense or audible artifacts.

Implement any new audio generation without compromising frame stability.

## 17. World Generation and Persistence

Materials must be derived from stable generation inputs.

Do not store full generated texture maps in every world save unless necessary.

Prefer storing:

- Material recipe version.
- World seed.
- Relevant generation identifiers.
- Persistent environmental changes.
- Explicit overrides where necessary.

Preserve actual gameplay changes.

For example, if a player excavates a cave, the modified geometry and save state must remain intact.

A renderer upgrade must not erase historical world changes or alter unrelated simulation systems.

Generated material changes must respect world-generation versioning and existing save compatibility rules.

No silent destructive migration.

## 18. Rendering Performance

Use the existing hardware-performance expectations, including an RTX 3060 Ti 8 GB, i7-8700K, and 64 GB RAM as a practical validation target.

Prioritize smooth camera movement and stable frame pacing.

Investigate:

- GPU shader cost.
- Texture bandwidth.
- Shader variant counts.
- Procedural sampling frequency.
- Material batching.
- Mesh instancing.
- Streaming performance.
- CPU-to-GPU synchronization.
- Memory allocation.
- Shader compilation.
- Runtime generation spikes.

Don't automatically bake every procedural material into a full-resolution texture.

Use an evidence-based hybrid of GPU shader generation, selective caching, and conventional assets.

## 19. Visual Reference and Art Direction

Use the previously generated Aevoria overhead concept map as the intended art-direction reference.

The desired visual identity includes:

- Rich Scandinavian northern forests and mountains.
- Broad connected mountain systems.
- Natural temperate green regions.
- Detailed Mediterranean limestone coasts.
- Beautiful rolling countryside.
- Distinct desert landscapes.
- Geologically meaningful colors.
- Visually coherent biomes.
- Clear coastlines and terrain silhouettes.
- Detail that survives all camera distances.

The reference is a visual goal, not a fixed map layout that every seed must reproduce.

**Never substitute a static image of the concept map for actual generated and explorable geography.**

If the concept image isn't available in the project, report the missing reference rather than inventing access to it.

## 20. Implementation Phases

### Phase 0 — Preservation and profiling

Establish a verified baseline, protect existing content, and measure build size, memory use, and performance.

### Phase 1 — Procedural material foundation

Implement the versioned material recipes, shader architecture, deterministic seeds, and geographic inputs.

### Phase 2 — Rock material prototype

Produce a visually convincing limestone/rock material and demonstrate natural variation on an actual mountain.

### Phase 3 — Anti-tiling and blending

Eliminate obvious texture repetition, seams, square transitions, and cliff stretching.

### Phase 4 — Regional materials

Implement grassland, forest floor, desert, snow, and coastal surface families.

### Phase 5 — Geometry integration

Blend surface materials with scattered real-world detail objects and appropriate LOD systems.

### Phase 6 — Visual polish

Improve surface depth, roughness, large-scale color variation, and consistency between World Mode and Explorer Mode.

### Phase 7 — Performance and size optimization

Measure GPU and CPU costs, optimize material sampling, eliminate redundant data, and evaluate actual installation size.

### Phase 8 — Procedural audio prototype

Implement and compare compact procedural environmental sounds against conventional audio.

### Phase 9 — Full native validation

Run representative Windows builds, inspect real screenshots, verify persistence, and compare performance and install size against the baseline.

### Phase 10 — Final release

Publish the v0.0.14 release only after technical and visual acceptance criteria pass.

Use actual native Windows gameplay, not only mathematical tests or Unity Editor previews.

1. Mountain seen from a high-altitude world view.
2. Mountain viewed from a medium distance.
3. Mountain cliff viewed up close.
4. Mediterranean grassy hillside.
5. Forest floor.
6. Northern snowy mountains.
7. Desert dunes and salt flats.
8. Rocky coastline.
9. Riverbank and lake shoreline.
10. Adjacent terrain chunks with different materials.

For each location, test changes in camera altitude, rotation, pan, lighting, and LOD.

Look specifically for repeated patterns, visible squares, color discontinuities, shimmering noise, stretched surfaces, and abrupt loading transitions.

If defects remain, document and resolve them before claiming visual acceptance.

## 22. Acceptance Criteria

The update is successful only when:

- No obvious square texture tiling is visible in representative terrain screenshots.
- Materials transition naturally across world geography.
- Mountain rock looks substantially more detailed and believable.
- Grasslands, deserts, forests, and snowy terrain have distinct visual identities.
- Surface detail behaves appropriately at multiple viewing distances.
- No new chunk seams or rendering defects are introduced.
- Procedural generation is deterministic for identical inputs.
- Existing worlds, saves, characters, and gameplay are preserved.
- Performance remains acceptable and is measured rather than assumed.
- Installation size is measured and compared with the baseline.
- Procedural audio improves or preserves perceived quality without destabilizing the game.
- Native Windows tests and visual reviews are completed.
- No incomplete feature is described as production-ready.

## Final Directive

**Build Aevoria's terrain to look like real, naturally varied landscapes—not a collection of square textures.**

Use compact mathematical recipes wherever they produce equal or better quality. Favor realistic geological variation, continuous material transitions, and beautiful environmental detail.

Do not sacrifice the existing world simulation, player saves, or visual fidelity merely to minimize file size.

Start by inspecting the finished v0.0.13 baseline, then implement the procedural material system in the phased order above.
