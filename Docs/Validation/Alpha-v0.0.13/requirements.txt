# 1. MISSION

Begin development of **Aevoria Alpha v0.0.13 — The Mediterranean Update immediately**.

Do not wait for further approval of the Dr. House character model.

The user will review and request any remaining Dr. House facial corrections separately. Those corrections are deferred and are not prerequisites for this update.

This release must transform Aevoria from an artificial-looking procedurally generated environment into a beautiful, geographically coherent, massive living world.

The current world suffers from numerous problems:

- Mountains resemble sharp pyramids, cones, and disconnected needles.
- Geographic features appear randomly distributed rather than geologically related.
- Climate zones form excessively smooth or uniform bands.
- The terrain does not convincingly resemble Europe or Asia.
- Continents sometimes appear divided by oversized rivers rather than substantial oceans.
- The landscape has far too many tiny ponds and isolated water patches.
- Desert and grass materials appear grainy from above.
- Grasslands are too flat and visually barren.
- There is insufficient ground vegetation, including flowers, grass clumps, shrubs, twigs, and rocks.
- Northern spruce forests are effectively absent.
- Northern terrain often looks brown rather than lush, mossy, forested, or appropriately snow-covered.
- Clouds have disappeared or become unreliable.
- Attractive distant mountain silhouettes have been lost.
- Terrain sometimes disappears in the upper part of the screen during zooming.
- Village paths contain unnatural jagged turns.
- The world-generation preview is blurry and low quality.
- Interface text is clipped.
- The Escape menu is overcrowded.
- The current menu backgrounds use outdated imagery.
- World Mode lacks a compass.

This update must address these issues as a coordinated overhaul.

**Aevoria's environmental identity should be inspired by Europe, the Mediterranean, Scandinavia, Asia, and North Africa.**

The world must feel geographically formed, not randomly assembled.

# 3. THE FOREVER-WORLD PHILOSOPHY

Aevoria is designed around the concept of a persistent world that may continue for years of simulated history and countless hours of gameplay.

The user is not particularly concerned about initial world-generation time.

Prioritize long-term world quality, visual complexity, coherent geography, and simulation reliability over fast initial generation.

This means:

- More sophisticated generation algorithms are acceptable.
- Multiple erosion and refinement passes are encouraged.
- Detailed geological modeling is desirable.
- Complex terrain meshes are acceptable.
- Rich vegetation and persistent geographic detail are desirable.
- High-resolution geographic fields are acceptable.
- Additional generation stages are acceptable.
- Do not impose arbitrary disk-space limits.
- Do not optimize quality away merely to save storage.

However, reliability is still mandatory.

Generation must:

- Make observable progress.
- Remain cancellable.
- Detect stalled workers.
- Avoid infinite loops.
- Handle large-world memory responsibly.
- Provide honest loading progress.
- Persist completed worlds deterministically.

A world that takes several minutes to generate is acceptable if the result is significantly better.

A world that hangs indefinitely is not.

Once created, the world must run smoothly enough to explore and simulate on the target hardware.

# 4. NEW GEOLOGICAL AND MESH GENERATION ARCHITECTURE

The user wants **mesh-based geographic detail throughout the world**, not simply textures painted over flat terrain.

Implement a modular geography architecture based on deterministic data layers and specialized generated meshes.

## 4.1 Required generation systems

Create or enhance dedicated systems for:

- Continental structure.
- Ocean basins.
- Regional elevation.
- Geological uplift.
- Tectonic fault networks.
- Folded mountain belts.
- Individual mountain massifs.
- Secondary ridges.
- Hills and valleys.
- Plateaus.
- Escarpments.
- Cliffs.
- Geological fractures.
- River channels and banks.
- Lakes and lakebeds.
- Erosion and sediment.
- Coastal beaches.
- Fjords.
- Dune fields.
- Salt flats.
- Surface rock formations.
- Cave entrances and underground caverns.
- Forests and vegetation.
- Settlement placement.
- Roads, footpaths, and agricultural terrain.

Each module should have clearly defined inputs and outputs, versioning, deterministic seeding, and integration tests.

## 4.2 Authoritative shared terrain

Maintain one authoritative geographic surface and coordinate system.

Specialized mesh systems must agree with:

- Terrain elevation.
- Physics collision.
- Water boundaries.
- Settlement foundations.
- Vegetation placement.
- World coordinates.
- Origin rebasing.
- LOD transitions.
- Save/load state.

Avoid producing disconnected meshes floating above, beneath, or through the terrain.

Climate and biome modules do not require separate visible geometry merely because they are independent data layers.

Mountains, cliffs, rock outcrops, river channels, dunes, cave entrances, and other physical features may use specialized meshes when appropriate.

Use a unified terrain mesh for broad continuous landforms, with additional geometry for features that require actual three-dimensional shape.

Avoid duplicate overlapping terrain meshes, z-fighting, and unnecessary GameObjects.

## 4.3 Generation dependency ordering

Use a layered pipeline approximately as follows:

1. World seed and size.
2. Continental and ocean geography.
3. Tectonic regions and fault networks.
4. Regional elevation and major uplifts.
5. Mountain formation and hill systems.
6. Valleys, basins, plateaus, and escarpments.
7. Large-scale erosion and drainage conditioning.
8. Ocean, river, and lake generation.
9. Climate, weather, temperature, and moisture.
10. Biome distribution.
11. Soil, surface materials, and geological details.
12. Vegetation and ecological placement.
13. Caves and special landforms.
14. Settlement and agricultural geography.
15. Mesh refinement, collision, and streaming products.
16. Final world preview and preparation for gameplay.

Iterate between erosion, hydrology, and other coupled systems where required.

The resulting geography should form a coherent whole rather than unrelated layers.

## 6.2 Different mountain formations

Support several distinct types.

**Mountain massifs**

One dominant high summit, approximately three or four major subordinate peaks, connected ridges, valleys, and foothills.

**Mountain belts**

Longer interconnected ranges with multiple summits, passes, and valleys.

**Coastal mountains**

Steep Mediterranean mountains descending toward the sea, with occasional sea cliffs and rugged shorelines.

**Older eroded mountains**

Broader and more rounded landforms with weathered ridges.

**Extreme fantasy mountains**

Very tall formations that remain geographically integrated instead of looking like vertically stretched cones.

## 6.3 Mountain shapes

Generate:

- Irregular ridgelines.
- Subsidiary peaks.
- Rock faces.
- Natural cliff bands.
- Broad foothills.
- Mountain saddles.
- Traversable mountain passes.
- Steep valleys.
- Glacial valleys in cold regions.
- Drainage channels.
- Exposed rocky slopes.
- Snowlines appropriate to elevation and climate.

Avoid:

- Single-polygon-looking summits.
- Uniform triangular pyramids.
- Needle-shaped formations.
- Repetitive cones.
- Disconnected foothills.
- Giant vertical seams.
- Rounded inflated blobs.
- Repeating ridges across an entire continent.

## 6.4 Extreme elevation preservation

Keep the mountain-relief settings functional.

Support mountains of approximately 20,000 meters and rare extreme mountains around 30,000 meters or higher where intentionally configured.

These are fictional extreme elevations.

The correct solution is to develop a wider supporting geological mass and regional structure, not simply stretch a small mountain vertically.

At ordinary relief settings, mountains should remain appropriately scaled.

Ordinary hills must remain much smaller and more gently sloped.

## 6.5 True physical terrain

The mountain rendering, collision, and surface coordinates must agree.

A mountain must not visually exist in a different place from its collider.

Players must be able to:

- Walk on its slopes.
- Fly around it.
- Land on it.
- Mine appropriate surface rocks.
- Travel through its valleys.
- Enter nearby caves where generated.

Validate at multiple LOD levels.
